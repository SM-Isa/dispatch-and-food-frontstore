## 29.3 Section 1 — Welcome and Role Guide

### 1.1 Who we are
Dispatch is a Nigerian food delivery platform built specifically for tier-2 cities. We're starting in Ilorin (Tanke, GRA, Challenge, Fate) and will expand to more cities in 2027.

We're not trying to be the next Uber Eats. We're building the food delivery platform that tier-2 Nigeria actually needs — one that works for the informal vendor, the prepaid customer, and the local rider.

Our values:

Trust over speed. A slow delivery that arrives is better than a fast one that doesn't.

Vendors are partners, not numbers. Every vendor we onboard, we support through their first 30 days and beyond.

Riders are the face of the platform. Every customer meets a rider. Their experience is our reputation.

Cash discipline. Every naira is tracked. We don't spend money we haven't earned.

Data over opinion. If it's not measured, it's not managed.


### 1.2 What we're building
At the end of 12 months:

200 active vendors

200 active riders

10,000 monthly active customers

25,000 orders/month

Profitable on unit economics

At the end of 3 years:

3 cities in Nigeria

800 vendors

600 riders

₦3.2B annual GMV

This is a real business. Not a side project. Not a pitch deck. Real vendors earning real money, real riders making real deliveries, real customers getting real food.


### 1.3 Your role
Vendor Ops Coordinator:
You own the vendor relationship from first contact through the first 30 days. You onboard vendors, support them, and keep them active. You're the vendor's first call when things go wrong.

Rider Ops Coordinator:
You own the rider relationship from application through active delivery. You verify guarantors, run training sessions, and manage the rider community. You're the rider's first call when things go wrong.

Field Coordinator:
You make sure every field visit happens on time. You schedule visits, collect documents, log interactions, and support the team. You're the operational glue.

Growth Lead:
You own customer acquisition. You run WhatsApp campaigns, field activations, referral programs, and paid social. You track CAC by channel and adjust spend weekly.


### 1.4 Your daily rhythm
07:00 — Office. Review yesterday's metrics. Read WhatsApp groups. Check the dashboard.

09:00 — Morning standup (15 min). Field assignments for the day. Blockers.

09:30 — Field work begins. Vendor visits or rider verification or customer campaigns.

13:00 — Lunch break (45 min). Return calls. Log morning interactions.

14:00 — Field work resumes.

17:00 — Return to office. Debrief with team. Log afternoon interactions.

18:00 — End-of-day sync (30 min). Team shares what happened.

18:30 — Support calls. Respond to vendor/rider issues from the day.

19:00 — End of shift. Tomorrow's assignments sent.


### 1.5 How you're measured
Every ops team member is measured on these five metrics:

Completion of daily assignments — did you visit the vendors/riders you were assigned?

Quality of interactions — did the vendor/rider feel supported after you left?

Data accuracy — is every interaction logged correctly?

Response time — how fast do you answer vendor/rider calls?

Retention — are the vendors/riders you onboarded still active 30 days later?

Weekly review: Every Monday, your manager reviews your numbers with you. Honest conversation. What's working, what's not.

Monthly review: Formal review with targets and stretch goals.


### 1.6 What you don't do
You don't set policy. Commissions, rider pay, delivery fees — these are set by the founder team.

You don't promise things you can't deliver. "I'll get back to you" is better than "yes" that later becomes "no."

You don't argue with vendors/riders. If they're upset, listen. Escalate. Don't fight.

You don't discuss confidential information. Vendor earnings, rider issues, customer disputes — these stay between you, the vendor/rider, and your manager.

You don't take gifts over ₦5,000 from vendors or riders. A bottle of water is fine. A suitcase of rice is not.

## 29.5 Section 3 — Vendor Playbook (Condensed)
Note: The full playbook is Artifact 14. This is the field version, focused on the daily work.


### 3.1 Vendor profile
Sign these:

Fixed location

5–25 menu items

Owner present most days

Already has customers

Average order ₦1,500–5,000

Accepts bank transfer (or willing to learn)

Decline politely:

No fixed location

Street food

Refuses menu photos

Bad hygiene reputation

Menu > 40 items

Owner never present

Do not decline harshly. Say: "You don't meet our current requirements. Feel free to reapply in 6 months if things change."


### 3.2 The 60-second pitch
"Good afternoon. My name is [X], I work with Dispatch — we're a food delivery app that just launched in Ilorin.

We bring you customers who want your food but can't come to you. You don't pay us anything to sign up. We take a small commission only when we deliver an order.

You keep your kitchen, your prices, your customers. We handle the delivery and the payments.

Can I show you how it works? It takes 5 minutes."

Rules:

Speak Nigerian English, not Pidgin, not too formal

Lead with what they get, not what they do

The word "commission" is fine, "fee" is not

End with a question, not a statement


### 3.3 The 5-minute pitch
Anchor on their problem (60s): "You know how customers call and then don't come because of traffic?"

Show the app (90s): Open vendor portal. Show a real menu. Play the notification sound.

Explain the economics (60s): "₦2,500 jollof. Customer pays ₦2,500. You keep ₦1,950. Rider gets ₦580. We keep ₦550."

Handle objections (90s): See Section 3.4

Close (60s): "Let's do this. I'll take a photo of your menu, upload it, you're live tomorrow."


### 3.4 Objection handling
"I already have enough customers."

"This isn't about replacing them. It's about the customers who want your food right now but can't come. Those exist."

"How do I know you'll pay me?"

"You don't pay us to sign up. When a customer orders, money comes to us. We pay you within 24 hours. You see every transaction in the app."

"15% is too much." (Note: we charge 20% base, but frame around value.)

"If you normally sell 20 plates a day, and Dispatch brings 5 more, you're making ₦10,000 extra. Without us, those 5 plates don't happen."

"I don't trust these apps."

"Fair. Here's what's different: we're doing Ilorin properly, cluster by cluster. Here's my phone number. Call me if you ever have a problem."

"What if a customer complains?"

"We handle complaints. That's our job. You just cook."


### 3.5 Onboarding flow (12 steps)
Open vendor portal at vendor.dispatch.ng/signup

Enter phone number → send OTP → vendor enters OTP

Fill profile: store name, cluster zone, address, hours, bank account

Verify bank account via Monnify resolution

Take storefront photo

Select commission tier (default 20%; promo rates available)

Capture menu (photo or manual)

Gemini extracts menu (5–8 seconds)

Review with vendor — correct prices, categories

Photograph top 3 dishes

Place test order (₦1,000–2,000 from your account)

Complete training (20 min, see Section 3.6)

Total time: 45–60 minutes.


### 3.6 Menu photo guide
Good photo:

Natural light, no flash

Straight down or flat-on (no 45° tilt)

Full menu in frame, all edges visible

Sharp text, in focus

Clean background

Bad photo:

Flash reflection

Tilted

Cropped edges

Blurry

Cluttered background

The photographer's checklist:

Hold phone 12 inches above menu

Hold phone flat (top edge points away)

Wait for autofocus (tap screen on text)

Check edges are in frame

Check light on menu, not on background

Take 2 shots (one straight, one angled)

If menu is on chalkboard: photograph before it's wiped. Chalk fades.

If menu is on wall: photograph from best angle. If unreadable, ask vendor to write on paper.

If menu is only in vendor's head: dictate to notes app. Read back. Transcribe to paper. Photograph.


### 3.7 When Gemini fails
Failure modes and fixes:

Image unreadable — retake photos per guide. If still failing, use manual entry.

Ambiguous prices — read each price aloud to vendor. "Jollof Rice — two thousand five hundred. Right?" Vendor confirms.

Wrong categories — manually override. Log a bug in #ai-quality if 5+ wrong.

Vendor refuses photo — explain it's for the customer. Offer to review before publishing. Skip if still refusing.

Gemini down — portal switches to manual entry. Use it.


### 3.8 First 7 days of vendor support
Day 1 (evening): Call vendor. "Did you get any orders today? Any issues?"

Day 2: No contact unless issue.

Day 3: WhatsApp check-in. "How's it going? Any questions?"

Day 5: Review performance dashboard. Green (5+ orders), Yellow (2–4), Red (0–1).

Day 7: In-person visit. Review earnings. Show top items. Plan next week.


### 3.9 Churn signals and saves
Tier 1 (churn within 7 days):

2+ days closed during normal hours

Acceptance rate < 60% for 3 days

Zero orders for 5 days (was doing 3/day)

Action: Call within 4 hours. Diagnose. Offer specific fix.

Tier 2 (churn within 30 days):

Order count declining 3 weeks in a row

Acceptance rate 60–80% for 2 weeks

50% of items marked sold-out for 5+ days

Action: WhatsApp with data. Suggest specific improvement.

The save playbook:

Call

Diagnose (don't pitch)

Offer a specific fix, not a general plea

Follow up in writing

Log outcome


### 3.10 Vendor-specific pain points
"Food is cold when it arrives."

Check: is the rider picking up too early? Is the delivery bag insulated?

Fix: coach the vendor to mark ready only when food is actually ready. Coach rider to use the bag.

"Rider came late."

Check: dispatch time vs. arrival time.

Fix: report to dispatch engine team. If chronic, escalate.

"Customer complained about portion."

Check: does the portion match the photo?

Fix: encourage vendor to update photos if portion changed.

"My menu isn't showing up in search."

Check: is_published = true. is_open = true. Cluster zone correct.

Fix: republish menu, verify.

"I'm not getting orders."

Check: is the vendor in a cluster with demand? Is the menu priced competitively?

Fix: feature vendor in the app's homepage for a week. Advise on pricing.

### 4.5 Safety briefing
30 minutes. Non-negotiable. Delivered in person.

3+ liters of water daily

15-min break every 4 hours

Eat properly

Customer safety:

Food bag: closed, upright, clean

Sealed containers: check for tampering

Never hand food to a child under 12

## 29.7 Section 5 — Incident Response Cards
Each card is one page. Print on cardstock, cut, laminate.


#### Card 1 — Customer is angry
Immediate actions (first 60 seconds):

Listen without interrupting

Say: "I understand. Let me help."

Take notes (order number, issue, what they want)

Questions to ask:

Order number?

What specifically happened?

What would make this right for you?

Resolve or escalate:

If resolvable (refund, redelivery): resolve now

If not resolvable: promise callback within 2 hours, escalate to ops manager

Never:

Argue

Blame the rider or vendor

Promise something you can't deliver


#### Card 2 — Rider accident
Immediate actions:

Call 112 (emergency) or the nearest hospital

Call rider ops coordinator (24/7 hotline)

Call CTO if life-threatening

Dispatch another rider for the delivery if possible

Within 30 minutes:

Notify rider's emergency contact

Start incident report in CRM

Take photos if safe to do so

Within 24 hours:

File insurance claim

Follow up with hospital

Update rider's family

Never:

Leave the scene without ensuring care

Discuss liability

Post on social media


#### Card 3 — Vendor is out of stock
Immediate actions:

Apologize to customer

Offer alternatives from same vendor or different vendor

If customer declines: full refund

Within 2 hours:

Message vendor: "Please mark [item] as sold out"

Coach vendor: "This happened because [reason]"

Log incident

Follow-up:

If recurring, escalate to vendor ops lead


#### Card 4 — Order is very late (>45 min past promise)
Immediate actions:

Check rider location in admin

Call rider — what's happening?

If stuck (traffic, bike issue): dispatch alternate rider

Notify customer with realistic ETA

Compensation:

If late by 30–60 min: ₦200 credit

If late by 60+ min: ₦500 credit + item discount

If 90+ min: full refund + item credit

Follow-up:

Log reason (rider issue, vendor issue, weather, traffic)

If chronic, escalate


#### Card 5 — Food quality complaint
Immediate actions:

Listen fully

Apologize

Ask: "Do you want a refund or a replacement?"

Policy:

Full refund if food is inedible

Replacement if food is wrong but edible

50% refund if food is cold but edible

Follow-up within 4 hours:

Contact vendor

If food safety concern (poisoning): suspend vendor pending investigation

File report with Kwara State Ministry of Health if serious


#### Card 6 — Rider robbed
Immediate actions:

Ensure rider is safe (call them, check location)

Call police if rider is at scene

Pay rider's lost income (₦15,000 for the day)

Arrange replacement bike if needed

Within 24 hours:

File police report

File insurance claim

Take rider off the app for 3 days (recovery)

Follow up with family


#### Card 7 — Vendor complains about commission
Immediate actions:

Listen fully

Explain: "Our 20% commission covers payment processing, customer support, marketing, and delivery logistics."

Ask: "What would make this work for you?"

Escalation:

If vendor is anchor (> ₦500K/month GMV): escalate to vendor ops lead

If vendor threatens to leave: escalate to CEO

Never:

Unilaterally change commission

Promise a rate reduction

Discuss other vendors' rates


#### Card 8 — App is down
Immediate actions:

Check status page

Notify CTO immediately (P1)

Post on WhatsApp groups: "We're aware. Working on it."

Take orders manually if critical

During outage:

Update status page every 15 min

Respond to every customer/rider/vendor inquiry

Do NOT post on social media (refer to official channels only)

After recovery:

Post-mortem within 48 hours

Notify affected users with apology

Compensate if appropriate


#### Card 9 — Food safety incident
Immediate actions:

Ensure customer's medical needs are met (₦200K coverage)

Suspend vendor immediately

Notify CEO within 1 hour

File report with Kwara State Ministry of Health within 24 hours

Within 48 hours:

On-site inspection (ours or state's)

Interview vendor's staff

Review food handling practices

Within 7 days:

Decision: reinstate vendor or remove permanently

Public statement if warranted (with legal review)


#### Card 10 — Police stop rider
Immediate actions:

Rider calls ops hotline

Ops asks: officer's name, station, badge number, reason

Ops calls company lawyer

Never:

Rider pays "settlement"

Rider runs

Rider argues

If bike is impounded:

## 29.12 Section 10 — Glossary
Every internal term, defined.

Anchor vendor — A vendor with > ₦500K/month GMV, getting priority support and lower commission (18%).

AOV — Average Order Value. Currently ₦4,450.

CAC — Customer Acquisition Cost. Blended currently ₦2,200.

Cluster — A geographic zone (Tanke, GRA, Challenge, Fate).

Contribution margin — Revenue minus variable costs, per order. Currently ₦920.

Dispatch engine — The system that offers deliveries to riders.

ESOP — Employee Stock Ownership Plan. Currently 10% of shares reserved.

GMV — Gross Merchandise Value. Total value of orders.

Guarantor — A person who vouches for a rider. Required for onboarding.

Idempotency — The property that repeating an operation has the same effect as doing it once.

KYC — Know Your Customer. Verified identity.

Ledger — The double-entry accounting system tracking all money movement.

LTV — Lifetime Value. Currently ₦12,144 over 12 months.

NDPR — Nigeria Data Protection Regulation.

North star metric — Orders per day. The single metric that matters most.

Offers — Delivery requests sent to riders.

OMS — Order Management System.

PSP — Payment Service Provider (Monnify, Korapay).

Rider captain — An elected leader of a cluster's rider community.

Round — A dispatch attempt (Round 1 = nearby riders, Round 5 = manual).

SLA — Service Level Agreement. E.g., 30-minute delivery.

Tier 1/2/3 — Vendor/rider quality segments.

Vertex AI — Google Cloud's ML platform, used for demand forecasting.

WHT — Withholding Tax.

Zero-sum — The ledger invariant: all debits equal all credits.
